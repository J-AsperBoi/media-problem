# red-at-the-edge

**Feature:** saturation isolation / colour at the frame edge. Group: colour.

**What the viewer should feel:** the eye jumps to the red at once; unease. A single odd colour "pops out" in parallel with no search, however busy the frame, and the odd one out is remembered better (FILM_GRAMMAR.md §1 finding 2: Treisman & Gelade 1980 feature integration; von Restorff; High). The viewer sees danger Ari hasn't = suspense (§5 "The hook" recipe, [S31]).

**Exact parameters used**
- Duration 4.0 s, 1080x1920, 30 fps. Locked-off camera.
- Wide: Ari full figure at zoom 1.0 (scale 1, feet at 540,1535, 1150 px tall), `calm`, looking away (look [-0.35, 0.1]), no fragment, no green anywhere. Everything else gray.
- Red `#ff3b30` enters the right edge at 0.5 s; leading edge creeps left at 20 px/s (70 px deep by 4.0 s), spanning y 760-1920 with a rounded top and a slow noise wobble (±9 px), plus a 36 px soft red glow ahead of the edge.
- Measured red area at 3.99 s: 4.2% of frame (limit 5%).
- Sound: quiet drone only. No text.

**Variants worth comparing**
1. Faster creep (40 px/s) vs 20 px/s: more alarm, but is it still "at the edge"?
2. Red confined to a small patch (y 1300-1920, street level) vs the tall band: how small can it be and still grab the eye?
3. Ari holding a glowing green fragment (glow 1.0) so red and green share the frame: does a second saturated colour dilute the pop-out?
