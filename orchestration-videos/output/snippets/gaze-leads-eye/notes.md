# gaze-leads-eye (snippet #10)

**Feature:** gaze cue / eyeline + POV (group: acting / edit).

**What the viewer should feel:** your eyes go where Ari looks; you see what they see. People automatically follow a face's gaze, even when told it is useless (FILM_GRAMMAR.md §2 "Eyelines / gaze direction", S23 Friesen & Kingstone 1998, High), and the cut to the looked-at thing then feels natural (§2 "POV", S3 High). Pupils lead, head follows 0.12 s later (§2.9 "Gaze").

**What's on screen:** 0–2.0 s: close-up, Ari framed slightly left (x 470) to leave look room on the right. Calm, looking ahead. At 0.80 s the pupils saccade right (0.07 s, to look 0.95); at 0.92 s the head follows, turning to turn 0.3 (~25°, since 0.55 ≈ three-quarter) over 0.5 s with a small tilt; at 1.1 s the face goes calm → notice. Hard cut at 2.0 s to a locked POV from 1.6 m eye height: a perspective street of gray buildings, four gray people standing in it, and a red wall filling the street end with a red spill on the road, creeping from 72 m to 64 m away over the hold.

**Sound:** steady drone only (no fades); gaze is the feature, so no cue on the cut.

**Params:** DUR 4.0 s; saccade 0.80–0.87 s (inOut), look x 0 → 0.95; head at 0.92 s, turn 0 → 0.3, headTilt 0 → 0.03, blend 0.5 s inOut; expression notice at 1.1 s; cut 2.0 s; POV focal 900 px, eye 1.6 m, vanishing point (560, 880), street half-width 7 m; red at z 72 → 64 m, 12 m tall.

**Weak spots:** the head turn is a 2D approximation (character.js 3/4 view), so 25° reads more as "features slide right" than a true turn. The red at the street end is small (~260 px wide) by design, so it depends on the red-pops-out effect.

**Variants worth comparing:**
1. Eyes only, no head turn (is the pupil shift alone enough to steer the viewer?).
2. Head lead delay 0.12 s vs 0.4 s (a slower, more deliberate look).
3. Cut at 1.4 s (sooner after the look) vs 2.0 s, or add a 'whoosh' on the cut.
